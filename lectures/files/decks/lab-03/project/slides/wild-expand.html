See what the shell really passes

student@server: ~/oslab-work/lab3

$ mkdir -p practice

$ printf 'one\n' > practice/report1.txt

$ printf 'two\n' > practice/report2.txt

$ printf 'log\n' > practice/report.log

 

$ printf '<%s>\n' practice/*.txt

<practice/report1.txt>

<practice/report2.txt>

$ printf '<%s>\n' 'practice/*.txt'

<practice/*.txt>

printf prints each word it receives between angle brackets, one per line.

Without quotes, the shell sends two words: the two .txt files. With single quotes around the whole pattern, it sends one word with a literal *.

Use this trick to check a pattern before you give it to cp, mv or rm.
