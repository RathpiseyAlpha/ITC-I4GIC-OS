grep, cut and awk

grep: keep matching lines

  • grep -v: lines that do not match.
  • grep -c: only the count of matching lines.
  • grep -i: ignore upper and lower case.
  • grep -E: extended patterns.

cut: simple fields

  • -d sets the delimiter, -f picks the fields.
  • cut -d: -f1 /etc/passwd lists account names.
  • It cannot do arithmetic or conditions.

awk: fields with logic

  • $1 is the first field, $2 the second.
  • END runs once, after the last line.
  • awk -F: '$3 >= 1000' /etc/passwd keeps ordinary accounts.

Use cut for simple delimited fields. Use awk when you need a condition or a sum.
